unicorne · unibody Corne · rev 0.10 · PCB placed, routing

16° per half, trackpad in the middle

Key spacing, column stagger, thumb cluster and LED positions are copied from the Corne v3 PCB (crkbd v3-final, 19.0 mm pitch), and each half is rotated 16°. The nice!nano is on the underside (dotted) with its USB port on the top edge, level with the inner key columns. The Azoteq TPS43 trackpad is centred in the open area between that edge and the bottom of the board. The halves sit as close as they can without a keycap coming within 2 mm of the trackpad. Like the original, the outer pinky columns snap off along the dashed lines to make a 36-key board. The outline runs 1.5 mm outside the switches and follows the column stagger.

Keycaps

Trackpad

Azoteq TPS43 (IQS572), reserved as 43 × 43 mm. The datasheet says 43 × 40 mm and one distributor lists 43 × 43 mm, so the larger size is kept clear until the exact part is chosen.

  • Talks I²C, so it shares the reserved bus on D2 / D3.
  • Its ready line goes to D4 / P0.22; reset has a 10k pull-up, and SDA/SCL have 4.7k pull-ups.
  • No FPC connector: six bare pads (J2) take short wires to the module's own pads, in the module's order SDA, SCL, 3V3, GND, RST, RDY. That keeps the stack as thin as possible.
  • The nice!nano is under the trackpad, so its header pins must be trimmed flush on the top side, or the trackpad needs a spacer.
  • Battery: through-hole B+ / B− pads below the trackpad, reachable from either side. The cell itself can go on the underside under the trackpad, below the nice!nano.

Exposed pads

Through-hole pads, so they can be soldered from either side. Labelled in copper on the drawing.

The battery pads (4.8 mm apart) sit below the trackpad, the trackpad pads above them, and the sensor pads 5 mm higher, just inside the trackpad area. A PAW3395 or PMW3360 board replaces the trackpad: fit one or the other, and leave the SDA/SCL pull-ups (R3, R4) off with a sensor. Both headers are on the underside next to the nice!nano, 2.54 mm pitch. Any 3 or 4 of the expansion pins can carry SPI instead, since the nRF52840 can route SPI to any pin.

Matrix

6 rows × 7 columns = 42 keys on 13 pins, COL2ROW diodes. The left half uses rows 0–2 and the right half rows 3–5. Columns 0–5 are the finger columns, counted from the outer pinky on both halves, so each column net crosses the centre once. Column 6 holds the three thumb keys, one per row.

The matrix uses the three low-frequency pins in the middle of the nice!nano (P1.01, P1.02, P1.07), which is fine for scanning keys. P0.24 and P0.11 stay free; P1.00 switches the LED supply. The 36-key version just leaves column 0 empty, so the same firmware works on both.

LEDs

  • One data chain from D1 / P0.06 through solder jumper JP1. Leave it open and the pin is a plain reserved pin.
  • Chain order: 12 underglow LEDs, then the per-key LEDs of the inner five columns and thumbs, and the two outer pinky columns last. Breaking those columns off, or leaving the per-key LEDs out, never cuts off an LED that is still fitted.
  • Per-key SK6812MINI-E exactly as on Corne v3, 4.75 mm south of each switch. Underglow uses Corne's six spots per half, but with 3.5 mm SK6812MINI LEDs: a 5 mm WS2812B doesn't fit between a hotswap socket and the next key's LED cutout. On the Choc board they sit 5.5 mm west of those spots, clear of the larger Choc sockets.
  • Their own switched supply: an AO3401A P-MOSFET, driven through a 2N7002 from P1.00 (high = on). The nice!nano's VCC stays on for the trackpad, and idle LEDs draw nothing.
  • Solder jumper JP2 picks the source: VCC (3.3 V, up to the nice!nano's 1 A regulator; cap brightness in firmware) or EXT, the LED IN pads, for an external supply up to 5 V and full brightness. Never the battery: RAW is the charger's battery pin.
  • With EXT, fit the SN74LV1T125 buffer (U2) so 3.3 V data drives LEDs at 5 V. With VCC, leave U2 and C2 off and bridge JP3, which bypasses it.

Two hotswap versions

Both use this placement on Corne's 19.0 mm spacing; only the switch footprint changes.

  • MX: Kailh MX hotswap sockets, MX keycaps.
  • Choc: Kailh Choc hotswap sockets. On MX spacing, Choc caps leave wider gaps (toggle above).

The two sockets overlap under the switch, so one board can't carry both. The Choc socket reaches further out, so on that board the diodes sit south-east of each switch and the break lines move clear of it.

nice!nano v2 pins

PinGPIOUse

"mid" pins are the three pads in the middle of the nice!nano (footprint pads 31–33), so the socket needs them too. P0.09 and P0.10 are NFC pins; ZMK needs CONFIG_NFCT_PINS_AS_GPIOS to use them. Exact row/column pin order gets fixed during routing.
